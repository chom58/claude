## ① Germany comparison → lift the *provisional* flag on Irish advantage
**Owner (source doc): Schyler**

「乳原料メーカーがどこに建てるか」を決める因子でドイツとアイルランドを比較すると、両者は **別の軸で勝っている**。

| Factor | Germany | Ireland |
|---|---|---|
| Milk pool | **EU's largest** — 22.0% of EU raw cows' milk (2024), 3.8m dairy cows (17% of EU); first in the EU for drinking milk, whey, butter and cheese | ~8.8bn L in 2025 (record), but smaller than Germany |
| Existing Japanese-owned processing base | **MILEI (Leutkirch / Allgäu), founded 1972, production from 1975** — WPC, lactoferrin, MPC, micellar casein; lactoferrin capacity expanded 2020 | Meiji technology licence into Danone Wexford (entry without building) |
| Integrated discovery-to-pilot chain | Dispersed (Thünen Institute, Max Rubner-Institut, etc.); Kerry also runs a Leipzig biotech hub | **FHI + Moorepark + VistaMilk + APC as one chain** |
| R&D cost incentive | Standard | **35% R&D tax credit, stackable with grants** |
| Entry mode | Owned / greenfield (Morinaga → MILEI acquisition) | **No-build** (technology licence / contract manufacture) |

**Proposed text** (replace the corresponding *Next steps* line; update the Score note):

> —**Germany wins on scale and owned commodity-ingredient manufacturing; Ireland wins on the discovery-to-pilot chain and a no-build entry route.** Germany is the EU's largest milk pool (22% of EU raw milk, 3.8m cows) and already hosts a Japanese-owned processing template in MILEI, producing WPC, lactoferrin, MPC and micellar casein at Leutkirch since 1975. That is the right home for *owned commodity-ingredient* volume, which is why Morinaga went there in 2012. But this sector's opening is functional and bioactive ingredients, not commodity. On that axis Ireland's integrated Food for Health Ireland → Moorepark → VistaMilk → APC chain, its 35% R&D tax credit, and the proven licence-in route at Wexford give it the edge Germany does not match. **The comparison supports holding Irish advantage at 3 and removing the provisional flag.**

→ This closes the only provisional score. Total stays **27 / 64**, now evidenced. **Confirm with Schyler before removing the flag.**

## ② European category data for functional dairy ingredients specifically
**Owner (source doc): Chey**

Market opening=3 is currently scored on the blended sector (closed commodity + open ingredient). Figures that isolate the open ingredient layer:

- **Europe dairy-protein *ingredients* market: USD 14.8bn (2025) → USD 25.6bn (2032), CAGR 8.1%**
- Europe whey-protein ingredients: ~USD 428m (2025) → USD 554m (2030), CAGR 5.3% (narrow definition)
- Dairy holds **32.4% of the European functional-food market (2025)** — the largest segment

**Proposed text** (add to Market overview → Europe):

> —**The open segment scales faster than the sector.** The European dairy-protein *ingredients* market is put at USD 14.8bn in 2025 rising to USD 25.6bn by 2032 (8.1% CAGR), well ahead of commodity dairy, and dairy already holds 32.4% of the European functional-food market. Market opening should be scored on this ingredient layer, not the blended sector.

→ Supports holding, or nudging up, Market opening once commodity is stripped out.

## ③ Ireland's position on precision fermentation and lactose-free processing
**Owner (source doc): Schyler & Chey**

結論は分かれる: **ラクトースフリーは GAP 解消、精密発酵は GAP のまま** が正確。

**Lactose-free → upgrade to STRONG:**
- **Kerry opened its expanded Carrigaline (Cork) biotech manufacturing hub in April 2026**, producing lactase enzymes at industrial scale. The enzyme processes **>2m tonnes of milk a year**, reaches ~28m consumers, and supplies 200+ customers across 80 countries. Cork and Leipzig (Germany) hubs coordinate.
- ⚠️ Note: this is **Kerry Group** (the ingredients/biotech company) — distinct from **Kinisla** (formerly Kerry Dairy Ireland). Do not conflate.

**Precision fermentation (recombinant milk proteins) → remains a GAP:**
- No Irish scale-up of precision-fermented *milk proteins* identified. Leading capacity sits outside Ireland (e.g. Standing Ovation, FR — USD 34.2m Series B for casein). Kerry's fermentation makes an *enzyme* (lactase), not recombinant milk protein — different thing.

**Proposed text** (replace the two rows in the Enabling technologies table):

| Technology or process | What it does | Irish position |
|---|---|---|
| Lactose-free and specialty processing | Enzymatic treatment for lactose-reduced products | **STRONG** — Kerry's Carrigaline (Cork) biotech hub makes lactase at industrial scale, processing >2m tonnes of milk a year for 200+ customers in 80 countries (opened Apr 2026) |
| Precision fermentation | Produces milk proteins without the animal | **GAP** — no Irish scale-up of precision-fermented *milk proteins* identified; leading capacity sits outside Ireland (e.g. Standing Ovation, FR). The one genuine capability gap to flag to EI |

## ④ Japanese dairy market size figure
**Owner (source doc): Chey**

Fills the source doc's "figure still to be added":

- **Japan dairy market: ~USD 32.6bn (2025) (≈ ¥5tn), CAGR 4.44%, → ~USD 40.5bn by 2030** (Mordor Intelligence).
  - Estimates range wider (USD 61–63bn in broad-scope reports, IMARC / EMR) — a definition difference. **Use the Mordor ~USD 33bn as the base, note the range.**
- **Functional / fortified dairy sub-segment: USD 6.1bn (2025) → USD 8.4bn (2035)** (Future Market Insights) — the layer this profile is about.
- Anchor: Yakult probiotic dairy drinks **¥424.9bn (FY2024)**.
- Supporting: FY2026 raw-milk output forecast **−1.8% to 7.25m tonnes** (first decline in three years) — reinforces the "domestic base restructured, not expanded" driver.

**Proposed text** (replace the "figure still to be added" placeholder in Market overview → Japan):

> The Japanese dairy market is put at roughly USD 33bn (about ¥5tn) in 2025, growing at 4.4% a year toward USD 40bn by 2030; broader-scope estimates run higher. The functional and fortified sub-segment specifically is USD 6.1bn in 2025, forecast to reach USD 8.4bn by 2035 — the layer this profile is about. Raw-milk output is meanwhile forecast to fall 1.8% to 7.25m tonnes in FY2026, the first decline in three years, reinforcing that the domestic base is being restructured, not expanded.
